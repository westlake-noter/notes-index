## Overview

This two-volume set is a comprehensive lecture-note-style textbook from Westlake University's Department of Mathematics. It builds mathematics from the ground up—starting with logic and sets, moving through abstract algebra and topology, and culminating in differential geometry, measure theory, and Fourier analysis. The exposition is rigorous and proof-oriented, with a strong emphasis on universal properties, category-theoretic thinking (in Volume II), and the interplay between algebra and analysis.

The text is self-contained: it begins with logic and set theory, requiring only basic mathematical maturity. Every theorem is proved with careful attention to hypotheses, and algebra and analysis are developed together—groups, rings, and modules sit alongside topology, measure theory, and calculus. Volume II introduces category theory, universal properties, and multilinear algebra early, and covers Stokes' theorem, Haar measure, change of variables, and Fourier analysis on tori and the real line.

It is suitable for advanced undergraduates and beginning graduate students in mathematics, serving as a bridge from computational calculus and linear algebra to abstract algebra, topology, and analysis.

## Volume I: Fundamental Algebra & Analysis

### Table of Contents

1. **Basic Logic**
   - Statements, negation, conjunction/disjunction
   - Conditional and biconditional statements
   - Proof by contradiction
   - Exercises (truth tables, logical puzzles, number theory proofs)

2. **Set Theory**
   - Roster and set-builder notation
   - Subsets, set difference, power sets
   - Quantifiers, sufficient/necessary conditions
   - Union, intersection, Cartesian product
   - Russell's paradox

3. **Correspondence**
   - Correspondences and inverses, graphs
   - Image and preimage, range and domain
   - Composition, surjectivity, injectivity
   - Mappings, bijections, direct products
   - Restriction and extension

4. **Binary Relations**
   - Equivalence relations and quotient sets
   - Partial orders, total orders, monotonic functions
   - Bounds, suprema/infima, intervals
   - Well-ordered sets, order-completeness
   - Knaster–Tarski fixed point theorem, Cantor–Bernstein, Dedekind–MacNeille completion
   - Recursive construction, countability

5. **Groups**
   - Composition laws, neutral and invertible elements
   - Subgroups, homomorphisms, quotients
   - Universal homomorphisms, direct sums/products

6. **Rings and Modules**
   - Unitary rings, monoid actions, vector spaces
   - Submodules, universal properties, matrices
   - Linear equations, Gaussian elimination
   - Quotient modules and rings, free modules
   - Rank–nullity theorem, algebras, formal power series

7. **Limit**
   - Filters, order limits, partially ordered groups
   - Enhancement, absolute values, ordered rings

8. **Topology**
   - Topological spaces, convergence, continuity
   - Initial topology, uniform continuity
   - Closed subsets, completeness, compactness
   - Compact metric spaces, path connectedness

9. **Differential Calculus**
   - Landau symbols, differentiability
   - Multilinear mappings, convexity
   - Mean value theorems, higher differentials
   - Taylor's formula, Banach spaces
   - Local inversion, uniform convergence, power series
   - Directional differential

10. **Integral Calculus**
    - Differential 1-forms, primitive functions
    - Riesz spaces, semirings
    - σ-additive functions, measurable spaces
    - Monotone class theorem, measure spaces
    - Product measures

## Volume II: Fundamental Algebra & Analysis (II)

### Table of Contents

1. **Language of Category Theory**
   - Universes, categories, functors
   - Natural transformations, Yoneda lemma
   - Representable functors

2. **Bilinear Algebra**
   - Linear forms, bilinear forms, quadratic forms
   - Quadratic forms over real vector spaces
   - Sylvester's law of inertia

3. **Self-Adjoint Operators**
   - Inner product spaces, orthogonal projection
   - Adjoint of bounded linear mappings
   - Reduction of self-adjoint mappings
   - Linear isometry

4. **Multilinear Algebra**
   - Tensor product, tensor algebra
   - Symmetric algebra, exterior algebra
   - Linear independence criterion
   - Determinant, adjugate

5. **Haar Measure**
   - Topological groups, locally compact Hausdorff spaces
   - Radon measure, Haar measure
   - Change of variables

6. **Reduction of Endomorphisms**
   - Principal ideal domains
   - Cayley–Hamilton theorem
   - Reduction of linear endomorphisms
   - Dunford and Jordan–Chevalley decompositions
   - Behavior under conjugation

7. **Differential Geometry**
   - Differential forms, localization and mollification
   - Vector fields, submanifolds, tangent space
   - Partition of unity, Stokes' formula

8. **Fourier Series**
   - Jensen and Hölder inequalities
   - Haar measure on a torus
   - Convolution, Fourier series
   - Fourier series on the real line
   - Fourier transform
